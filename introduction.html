Where is it?

Karnataka is in the south-west of India, sitting on the Deccan Plateau with the Arabian Sea on one side and the Western Ghats running along the coast. It touches Maharashtra and Goa in the north, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh in the east, Tamil Nadu and Kerala in the south. The Kannada spoken here is one of the classical languages of India.

Two very different halves

Drive west and the plateau climbs into the Western Ghats, where rain falls in rivers and coffee grows under shade trees. Drive east and the land dries into red soil and fields of sugarcane and tobacco. The coastal strip, called Karavali, is all beaches, coconut palms and fish curry.

A state built by many rulers

The Badami Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas, the Hoysalas, the Vijayanagara Empire at Hampi, the Bahmani and Adil Shahi sultans at Bijapur, the Wodeyars of Mysore and later Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan all left monuments behind. This is why a single state can hold a Hindu temple, a Islamic dome and a Gothic palace in the same trip.

Culture you can hear and taste

Karnataka has two classical arts side by side: Carnatic music in the south and Hindustani music in the north. Yakshagana is a loud, colourful night-long dance-drama from the coast, while Yasas and Dollu Kunitha are drum dances. Everything is finished with a steel tumbler of filter coffee.

Why people keep coming back

One state gives you a UNESCO World Heritage site, a tiger reserve, a hill station that smells of coffee, waterfalls you can stand under, and some of the cheapest and best food in India. Bengaluru, the capital, is also India's technology hub, so the airport connects the state to the whole world.
